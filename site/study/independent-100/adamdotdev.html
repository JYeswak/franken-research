Relevance to our work

5 of 5 lenses: agents, orchestration, mcp

OpenCode is an MIT coding harness at very large scale (about 210k stars) that we already reference: an archived skill, our own Grok-first router plugin, and session connectors in cass and casr. It is the main open benchmark for OMP's harness choices. models.dev is an MIT model and pricing catalog that could feed cost routing.

Where it already touches our work: We have used opencode alongside our main harness, and our session-search tools read its sessions.

Scored 0 to 5 against the study’s relevance rubric: 5 means public, openly licensed work squarely in our lanes that we could study or adopt now, and 1 means little connection to our work. The score is one reviewer’s judgement of how much this public work bears on our own work. It is not a ranking of people and says nothing about the person or the quality of their work.

What we would study next

  1. Re-evaluate current OpenCode against OMP for fleet use (plugins, skills path, sessions, MCP), and retire or refresh the archived skill (github.com/anomalyco/opencode). Our opencode skill is archived and the router README predates current OpenCode; either refresh it or remove the stale references. Effort: medium.
  2. Use models.dev as the source for model capability and price data in agent cost tracking (github.com/anomalyco/models.dev). An MIT, frequently updated dataset; avoids hand-maintained price tables. Effort: small.

Before reusing anything: OpenCode and models.dev are MIT. anomalyco/rift has no license. His courses, podcast and terminal.shop are commercial. His personal repo good-repo (1264 stars) has no license.
