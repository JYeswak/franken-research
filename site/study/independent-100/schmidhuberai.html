What they publicly do

AI researcher who writes an AI blog of historical and technical notes, many issued as IDSIA technical notes. In it he attributes LSTM, Highway Nets, fast-weight programmers, adversarial curiosity, world models and early recursive self-improvement algorithms to work by his teams [Verified: people.idsia.ch/~juergen/blog.html]. The blog lists him as Director of the AI Initiative at KAUST from 2021 and as Chief Scientist of NNAISENSE [Verified: same page].

Relevance to our work

3 of 5 lenses: research, research-method

The RSI survey and the Gödel Machine lineage give the theory behind self-improving agent loops. His 'who invented X' notes are a strong example of prior-art tracing. EvoTorch is open under Apache-2.0 but falls outside our current lanes.

What we would study next

  1. Read the RSI technical note, focusing on the Gödel Machine's 'provably useful self-rewrite' criterion (people.idsia.ch/~juergen/recursive-self-improvement.html). It gives a formal bar for when a self-modification should be accepted. That bar applies to any loop in which agents rewrite their own skills or prompts. Effort: medium.
  2. Read his 'who invented X' notes as a template for a prior-art check (people.idsia.ch/~juergen/who-invented-transformer-neural-networks.html). The notes trace claims back to their dated primary sources, which is the check our rulebook wants before a [Verified] novelty claim. They argue his side of disputed priority claims, so treat them as one input, not a verdict [Inference]. Effort: small.

Before reusing anything: The blog is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial, share-alike. Its text cannot be reused in commercial content. EvoTorch is Apache-2.0.
