No public CI; verification runs on non-inspectable maintainer RCH hosts.
- **Three most important pieces of evidence (packet tiers):**
  1. **The `skipped_not_proven` gate on the 200-pane headline.** "The retained 200-pane figures are synthetic benchmark-lane results, not a qualified native or target-class operating envelope"; high-scale wording "held back until the target-class hardware gate signs a non-skipped artifact (**currently `skipped_not_proven`**)" [Verified, High]. Refusal-to-claim as a machine-readable signed state — the single most load-bearing honesty artifact in the packet.
  2. **The RC53 held-out failure published verbatim.** "The RC53 held-out run **failed** its capture and storage service-envelope checks; fitted rates do not establish saturated throughput or a passing performance gate" (CHANGELOG) [Verified quote, High]. Survives an independent rerun *because it refuses the claim* [Inference, High].
  3. **Measured asupersync coupling with correlated risk.** 74 distinct `runtime_async::` APIs across 259 files / 2,378 usage sites at HEAD, pinned `=0.5.0`, tokio banned at four layers (dependency/`deny.toml`, sealed `RuntimeProof` trait with Lean soundness model, test-layer guards, custom lints) [Verified, High] — and asupersync carries the *same* OpenAI/Anthropic rider, making the runtime-dependency and license-exclusion risks "a single correlated failure domain" [Inference, High].

## 3. CI/EVIDENCE BAR: what frankenterm demonstrates that other packets lack

- **Content-addressed, sigstore-signed release attestation bundles with per-category producing-bead provenance + a machine-readable claim registry + an attestation graph (headline claim → signed slot).** Verified schema, manifest, and claim-registry exist in-tree (`docs/attestations/`) [Verified exists, High]. *Who lacks it:* literally everyone else — `grep` finds "attestation bundle" in no other packet. The nearest rival is franken_node, which *consumes* the incumbent solution: "the project's own release pipeline uses cosign keyless signatures (`.sigstore.json`) for binaries" [Code-verified, High] — artifact signing, but no claim-level bundles tying each claim to producing work items and negative-evidence slots. frankenlibc's packet makes the gap explicit: "there are no signed attestations of any kind — no signed tags…, no Sigstore/cosign signatures, no SLSA provenance" — written *against* the best measurement-discipline project in the program, which still lacks what ft ships.
- **Negative-evidence slots *inside* release bundles + published held-out failures in the CHANGELOG (RC53 verbatim quote).** 23 packets keep negative-evidence ledgers (frankenmermaid's 726-row ledger, franken_numpy's 67,641-line ledger, frankenlibc's 41,205-line ledger — the *ledger* is a shared pattern, not the bar). What no other packet does is bind negative evidence into the signed release artifact itself and ship a machine-readable refusal-to-claim state: the round-9 ledger's 8 grep-able **retry-condition predicate** forms plus the signed `skipped_not_proven` gate. The `skipped_not_proven` token appears in exactly one packet: frankenterm.
- **The auto-stamper that documents its own drift.** `scripts/stamp-readme-counts.sh` with a `--check` advisory gate; README says "Counts are auto-stamped… and drift fast." Adjacent but weaker: franken_networkx's five auto-generated ledgers "fail CI on drift" [Code-verified, High] — that is *strict* where ft is advisory, so ft's bar here is honesty-about-lag, not enforcement; franken_markdown's `claims.tsv` + `check-claim-discipline.sh` ("marketing hygiene as CI") wires claims to proof scripts, which ft's stamps do *not* do. ft's bar is real but partial.
- **Build-enforced async discipline (sealed `RuntimeProof` trait + Lean-modeled soundness, four-layer tokio ban).** Verified at HEAD with `deny.toml` `[bans]` wired into `scripts/release-gates.sh` [Verified, High]. Adjacent: franken_snowflake's "cargo-tree admissibility gate that fails the build if a forbidden crate appears in any of the ~21 feature lanes" [Verified, High] — same dependency-ban idea, but ft's four layers (dep → type-system seal → test guards → custom lints) plus a formal soundness model is the most complete version found.
- **Forensic compiler triage embedded in config.** The ~35-line rustc-ICE forensics comment in `rust-toolchain.toml` (diagnosed as a toolchain bug via identical signatures in `toon_rust` and `charmed_rust` on the same day) [Verified in the clone, High] — no other packet embeds a root-caused compiler-diagnostic write-up inside a pinned toolchain file.

## 4. SHARED HURDLES

- **Bus factor 1 + no-contributions policy as a *permanent* ceiling.** ft is the most explicit ("by explicit stated policy," README-quoted), but the hurdle is program-wide: franken_ocr pairs the same policy with "CI workflows deleted from the tree at HEAD"; frankensim's packet names the compound as "a structural adoption ceiling that no amount of correct engineering can climb without a governance change." ft adds the sharper variant: the *users are the excluded parties* (see below).
- **The license rider.** ft's MIT+OpenAI/Anthropic rider is quoted verbatim and the packet names the irony: "the product's users are its license's excluded parties." The rider is FrankenSuite-standard (present in most of the 44), but the *bite* is shared at full strength by the agent-adjacent projects: franken_tts — "a non-OSI MIT+OpenAI/Anthropic-rider license that bars exactly the two AI labs building agent-voice infrastructure from even benchmarking it"; franken_whisper — the rider "withholds all rights — including benchmarking and analysis — from the two leading AI labs"; frankenlibc — "the MIT+Anthropic/OpenAI rider makes independent evaluation by the best-equipped labs a license violation [High]." Note the honest divergence: the two beads-viewer repos (beads-for-frankentui, beads_for_franken_engine) and two website repos (frankensim_website, frankensqlite_website) have *no license at all* (GitHub API `license: null`) — the inverse posture, "worse for adoption than the rider: the rider at least grants *some* parties rights."
- **Velocity vs review depth.** ft: 16,269 commits since 2026-01-18 with README stamps lagging core LOC by ~50% — "honest machinery, maintained at a speed that defeats even its own automation." franken_node's packet names the general law: "~20 commits/day from a single human means most commits cannot have received meaningful independent review — the bus factor and the review depth are the same fact viewed twice" [Inference]. franken_code_browser: 101 commits in a single day (2026-09-17) under the same single maintainer; franken_engine: 96 commits on Sep 15 alone; franken_numpy: 8,621 commits.
- **Correlated single-maintainer dependency risk.** ft's next step 5 quantifies it: 74 `runtime_async::` APIs × 2,378 usage sites pinned to asupersync `=0.5.0`, which shares ft's rider — "the runtime, the product, and the license exclusion are a single correlated failure domain." In-program analogs: franken_snowflake runs its entire transport on asupersync `=0.3.5` [Verified, High]; frankensqlite "runs its storage I/O on asupersync 0.5.0" [Verified in Cargo.lock, High] — the same single-maintainer runtime under multiple flagship dependents.
- **Pinned nightly + aging toolchain.** ft pins `nightly-2026-08-31`. Shared posture with franken_networkx ("Rust 2024, nightly") and the program's general nightly dependence; ft's packet explicitly names the decay path: "if velocity decays… the pinned nightly ages; the 3.4M-line tree becomes unmaintainable. Bit-rot within quarters, not years [Inference, High]."
