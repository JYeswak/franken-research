## 1. OWN-PACKET DISTILLATION

**Verdict:** **NODUS ring: Explore** [Inference, Medium]; **TRL 5** ("Components validated in the relevant environment (differential conformance vs the live NumPy oracle, 8,716 tests, 0-row divergence ledger) and released on crates.io — but CI is red at the pin, no production deployment exists, and performance numbers are 97% non-contract by the project's own audit" [Inference, Medium]).

**One-paragraph case (packet §4.12):** FrankenNumPy is a 402,564-line [Counted, High] bet that NumPy's behavior can be re-derived in safe Rust with machine-checked parity and published proof of every claim. On process honesty it outruns every peer: a 642-site unsafe census confined to the PyO3 boundary [Counted, High], 8,716 tests [Counted, High], a 67,641-line ledger of its own failures [Counted, High], a KEEP-claim audit that disavows 97% of its own benchmark claims [Maintainer claim, High], and a divergence ledger at zero rows [Code-verified, High]. But the product case is hollow at the pin: the 100% API surface is substantially NumPy re-exported (the wheel requires `numpy>=2.3` [Code-verified, High]), the speed numbers are non-contract by the project's own grading, CI is red at the pin on the lint gate while the badge says green [CI-observed, High], one human is the entire bus factor, and the license forbids the likeliest evaluators from even analyzing the code. "Explore the methodology — especially the differential harness and the ledger-hygiene gates; the software stays behind the rider's ceiling until trigger 3 fires." [Inference, Medium]

**Strongest strength:** the evidence-governance machinery — differential-against-live-oracle conformance harness (48 binaries [Counted, High], four test layers), ledger-hygiene CI gates, the KEEP-claim incumbent-coverage audit, and the 67,641-line negative-evidence ledger. The packet calls it "the most exportable artifact in the FrankenSuite" and the methodology-export thesis ("adopt the patterns, not the package") [Inference, High].

**Strongest ceiling:** the license rider — named-party discrimination barring OpenAI, Anthropic, affiliates, and anyone acting for them from use *including* "benchmarking, testing, analyzing, indexing" [Code-verified (license text), High]. It is instrumented as the advancement blocker: "Explore — substantive, unproven, and currently un-advanceable past Explore while the rider stands" [Inference, Medium], priced into feasibility (3/5) and collaboration (2/5).

**The 3 most important pieces of evidence:**

1. **The process claims verify to the unit; the outcome claims don't.** 8,716 `#[test]` functions counted with the README's own method, "exactly 8,716, matching the README figure to the unit" [Counted, High]; 10 of 11 crates `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` with zero counted unsafe sites, the 642 remaining sites all in `fnp-python` [Counted, High]. Meanwhile the README's own evidence grade admits that of 28 headline benchmark ratios, one (`isin` 134.5x) is contract-grade and ~22 are stock `cargo bench` reads — "Read the table as a map of where native kernels exist" [Maintainer claim, High]; the fleet KEEP audit puts it at 22/751 contract-grade (2.9%) [Maintainer claim, High]. The packet's summary: "claims about *process* verify at High confidence and mostly to the exact unit; claims about *outcomes* are either qualified by the maintainer's own evidence grading or depend on oracle runs this assessment did not execute" [Inference, Medium].
2. **CI is red at the pin while the badge says G1 green.** Run 35730374809 (head = pin `7de9ba73`, established 2026-09-22 via the Actions REST API): "G1 failed at `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` (`cargo fmt --check` and `cargo check` passed); G2–G9 all skipped" — so "the 499/499 lock, the differential gates, and the ledger-hygiene gates did not execute at the assessed commit" [CI-observed, High]. The README badge's "G1 green" reflects the 2026-09-03 state, not the pin [Code-verified, High]. "A project whose pitch is *auditability* failing its own lint gate at HEAD is a governance signal, not a typo" [Inference, Medium].
3. **The 100% surface is substantially re-export, and the wheel requires the incumbent.** `pyproject.toml` declares `dependencies = ["numpy>=2.3"]` [Code-verified, High]; the three-tier attribute model makes tier-3 names identity-equal re-exports — "`fnp_python.some_name is numpy.some_name` is literally `True`" [Maintainer claim, Medium]. "The tier-3 re-export architecture *structurally* requires a live NumPy; the dependency is load-bearing, not incidental" [Code-verified, High]. A "NumPy reimplementation" that cannot function without NumPy installed is "a compatibility layer with fast paths, not a reimplementation, for every workload that touches a tier-3 surface" [Inference, High] — and the per-name native-vs-re-export attribution is unaudited, which is why the tier-3 severance experiment is the packet's kill test.

## 3. CI/EVIDENCE BAR — what franken_numpy demonstrates that other packets lack

**E1 — The ledger-hygiene CI gate (G2).** numpy's 8-gate topology includes a dedicated gate that machine-checks the *schema of evidence rows*: G2's "machine-checkable `host=`/`worker=`/`harness=`/ELF-hash row contract" [Maintainer claim, High on the gate's existence and contract]. A targeted grep across all 44 packets confirms "ledger-hygiene" appears in **no other packet** — this gate is program-unique. Its value is demonstrated by the wound it would have prevented: frankenfs's packet records that "the August-2026 worker-scope audit admitted 166 published benchmark rows cannot prove which machine they ran on" [Verified, High] — the exact failure mode a machine-checked `host=`/`worker=` contract rules out at write time. frankenscipy has nine *green* gates at its pin, but none machine-checks ledger rows; franken_pandas has certified lanes with "ELF SHA pinning" [Code-verified, High] but applies it to lanes, not to every ledger row as a CI gate.

**E2 — The KEEP-claim audit with debt decomposition.** numpy's `docs/KEEP_CLAIM_INCUMBENT_COVERAGE.md` (fleet audit, 2026-07-31) grades 751 KEEP claims at 22 contract-grade (2.9%) [Maintainer claim, High] — and then goes one step further than any sibling audit: "a seeded hand-read of 30 of the 723 estimates ~554 do compare against a live NumPy arm but predate the 2026-07-26 schema" [Maintainer claim, High], i.e. it decomposes the debt into *schema debt* (re-recordable) vs *measurement debt* (needs fresh measurement) and scopes next-step work accordingly. franken_networkx's claim-coverage audit (2.0% incumbent-attested) and franken_mermaid's ("225 KEEP claims, 8 with a live incumbent ratio — 96.4% without") report the ratio but no packet evidence shows them decomposing the remainder into a scoped work plan. The honesty mechanism is family-shared; the *debt-classification sampling* is numpy's.

**E3 — The disavowal printed in the README itself.** numpy's README carries an "Evidence grade (audited 2026-09-02)" section stating that of 28 headline ratios only `isin` 134.5x is contract-grade, five are same-invocation ABBA rows with nulls, and ~22 are stock bench reads — "Read the table as a map of where native kernels exist" [Maintainer claim, High]. Siblings disavow in *docs*: frankenfs's parity report "explicitly disavows its own 97/97 number as 'not an executed-test result or a readiness score'"; franken_mermaid self-reports in its audit doc; franken_networkx's ledger contract says self-speedups "must not support a competitive claim" [Code-verified, High]. The packet's own comparative judgment ("the only one whose README prints its own evidence grade disavowing 97% of its benchmark claims" [Inference, Medium]) was made over a smaller completed set and should now be read as *strongest placement*, not sole practice — but numpy remains the packet where the disavowal sits in the most-read document, next to the numbers it disavows, plus the "Did You Know" museum-table: the 2026-05-25 cross-engine table "was measured after the FrankenNumPy side of every row had been switched to pass straight through to NumPy... The table is kept as the parity-era record; it is not a measurement of the FrankenNumPy engine" [Maintainer claim, High].

**E4 — The tier-attributed surface model with a designed severance kill test.** numpy documents a three-tier attribute model (native Rust fast-paths / native PyO3 classes / identity-equal re-exports) and — uniquely in the program — designed the falsifier for its own headline: "build a wheel with tier-3 re-exports severed and measure what fraction of `numpy.__all__` still resolves" (next step 2) [Inference, High — process proposal]. frankensympy documents delegation sites and franken_networkx maintains a delegation ledger, but neither packet proposes severing its delegation tier to quantify reimplementation share; "severance" appears in no other packet (grep-verified).

**E5 — The 499/499 structural lock test iterating the live incumbent.** `fnp_python_covers_full_numpy_all` "iterates the live `numpy.__all__` at runtime" and asserts reachability plus `fnp.__all__ == np.__all__` [Code-verified, High on the test's existence; the 499/499 figure is Maintainer claim, Medium]. franken_networkx machine-gates its surface too ("the generated export-route appendix machine-gates the full 843-name surface with `--check` failing on drift" [Counted, High]) — but against a *generated static list*, not the live incumbent: numpy's lock "holds as numpy evolves" by construction, and it is host-numpy-relative (501 names on numpy 2.3.x) [Code-verified + Maintainer claim, Medium]. The live-incumbent-relative lock is the bar-raising variant.

**Honesty about where numpy does NOT lead (other packets set the higher bar):**
- **Unsafe confinement:** numpy's 642-site census is analyst-recounted [Counted, High] but not CI-enforced. franken_lean's B8 machinery includes "a CI-enforced unsafe-site ledger" and "the bidirectional unsafe-ledger census passed CI at the pin" [CI-observed, High] — machine-checked beats hand-counted.
- **Dependency admissibility:** franken_snowflake enforces "a cargo-tree admissibility gate that fails the build if a forbidden crate appears in any of the ~21 feature lanes, dev included" [Verified, High]. numpy's numeric core is "dependency-light by design" [Code-verified, High] but the discipline is documented, not build-enforced.
- **Zero-unsafe posture:** franken_mermaid's "zero-`unsafe` claim is the strongest in this program — all 9 crate roots carry `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` and a code-level census finds zero actual `unsafe` tokens" [Verified, High]; franken_pandas has "a genuine zero-`unsafe` census — all 15 crate roots `forbid(unsafe_code)`" [Counted, High]. numpy confines but does not eliminate (642 sites in the PyO3 boundary).
- **Green multi-gate CI:** franken_scipy has "CI Gates G1–G9 green at the pinned commit" — the topology numpy aspires to but fails at G1.

## 4. SHARED HURDLES

## 5. GENUINE UNIQUENESS

Only things no other packet does (grep-verified where the claim is textual). Where numpy merely participates in a family pattern, it is labeled "also does X."

**U1 — The tier-3 severance experiment.** No other packet designs a kill test that severs its own delegation tier to quantify reimplementation-vs-re-export share. "severance" appears in zero other packets (grep-verified); the numpy next step is fully specified: "build a wheel with tier-3 re-exports severed and measure what fraction of `numpy.__all__` still resolves and what fraction of a real workload suite still passes... This quantifies reimplementation vs re-export — the packet's central qualification of the 100% claim — as a number instead of a table" [Inference, High — process proposal]. frankensympy and franken_networkx document delegation but propose no severance.

**U2 — The ledger-hygiene gate.** "ledger-hygiene" appears in zero other packets (grep-verified). numpy's G2 gate machine-checks the evidence-row schema (`host=`/`worker=`/`harness=`/ELF-hash) as a CI gate [Maintainer claim, High]. It is the program's only instance of *evidence-schema-as-CI-gate* — the mechanism whose absence produced frankenfs's "166 published benchmark rows cannot prove which machine they ran on" [Verified, High].

**U3 — The largest counted negative-evidence ledger in the program.** numpy: 67,641 lines [Counted, High]. Next-largest measured: franken_pandas 44,086 [Counted], franken_scipy 44,078, franken_torch 42,708, franken_libc 41,205, franken_networkx 37,116 [Counted, High], franken_whisper 26,846, franken_redis 26,485, frankenfs 20,556, franken_search 19,201, frankenjax 6,109. The *culture* is family-shared (~22 packets maintain one); the *scale* is numpy's alone.

**U4 — The three-tier surface model with a load-bearing incumbent dependency.** Delegation exists elsewhere (franken_networkx's 181 delegating functions; frankensympy's 4 `import sympy` delegation sites in the dev shell), but no other packet shows the incumbent as a *declared runtime dependency* that the architecture structurally requires: numpy's `pyproject.toml` declares `dependencies = ["numpy>=2.3"]` [Code-verified, High], and tier-3 names are identity-equal re-exports ("`fnp_python.some_name is numpy.some_name` is literally `True`" [Maintainer claim, Medium]). No sibling packet evidences a wheel that cannot install without its incumbent. (Caveat: absence of evidence in sibling packets is not proof no sibling does this — the claim rests on no packet *showing* it [Inference, Medium].)

**U5 — The deterministic stride-calculus engine as single shape authority.** `fnp-ndarray` (2,079 src lines [Counted, High]) owns `broadcast_shapes`, `element_count`, and stride computation under five documented invariants (element-count conservation, deterministic stride-from-shape, symmetric/associative broadcast legality, single-`-1` inference, view byte-span containment) [Code-verified, High on structure; Maintainer claim, Medium on exhaustiveness]. Domain-specific by nature, but within the 44-packet program no other project derives array shape/stride semantics as a standalone engine — frankenjax's nearest analog (JAXPR IR + tracing) is a transform-semantics substrate, a different thing.

**U6 — `fnp-random-core` as a portable bit-exact stream spec.** 328 lines, `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, dependency-free, "no I/O, no entropy, no threads" by design [Code-verified, High], with witness fixtures pinning every RNG distribution's output [Maintainer claim, Medium]. frankenjax has 25 RNG oracle fixture cases [Counted, High], but no sibling packet frames its RNG core as a *portable, versioned, cross-language stream spec* (numpy next step 4: "an independent implementation in a second language reproduces the checked-in witness vectors bit-for-bit") [Inference, Medium — the crate's portability is verified; demand for the spec is not].

**Explicitly NOT unique — "also does X":**
- **Bayesian runtime decision engine:** franken_engine runs a "Bayesian-posterior + e-process-boundary decision ladder (`allow / challenge / sandbox / suspend / terminate / quarantine`)" for runtime containment; franken_pandas has a "Bayesian runtime policy layer (`fp-runtime`)" computing "Bayesian posterior and expected losses"; franken_scipy's CASP does "runtime algorithm selection by Bayesian expected-loss minimization against calibrated loss matrices." numpy's instantiation (closed-form Bayesian posterior against a fixed loss model — silent-corruption cost 100.0, false-rejection cost 125.0 — with every decision logged to an `EvidenceLedger` [Maintainer claim, Medium]) is one of at least four Bayesian runtime-decision designs in the family.

## 6. CROSS-POLLINATION IN — concepts from other packets franken_numpy should adopt

**IN1 — Machine-checked claim discipline (`claims.tsv` + gate script) → from franken_markdown → why it fits → expected payoff.**
franken_markdown's "most original contribution is **methodological**: `claims.tsv` + `check-claim-discipline.sh` wire each README claim to a `capabilities --json` key and a proof script — marketing hygiene as CI, a discipline no incumbent practices" [Verified, High on the mechanism]. numpy's two live drift instances at the pin — the stale "G1 green" badge and "27 fuzz targets" vs the re-verified 30 [Code-verified, High] — are exactly README-claim-vs-code drift, and the packet's own open admission is that "the drift rate vs. audit rate is unmeasured" [Inference, Medium]. Wiring the README's numeric claims (test counts, fuzz-target counts, gate statuses, crate counts) to proof scripts that fail a check on drift would convert numpy's periodic audit cadence into continuous enforcement. Payoff: drift becomes a CI failure instead of a quarterly discovery; the "drift rate vs. audit rate" gap the packet names gets closed by construction. (Honesty note: franken_markdown's own packet flags that its enforcement runs through the maintainer's private DSR, "unobservable externally" [Verified, High] — numpy should wire the gate into the GitHub Actions topology it already has, not copy the DSR part.)

**IN2 — CI-enforced unsafe-site ledger → from franken_lean → why it fits → expected payoff.**
franken_lean's evidence-governance machinery includes "a CI-enforced unsafe-site ledger" that is "partly *already enforced* (the bidirectional unsafe-ledger census passed CI at the pin [CI-observed, High])". numpy's 642-site confinement is an analyst recount [Counted, High] — rigorous but episodic and external to the build. Adopting lean's B8 pattern (crate-root `forbid(unsafe_code)` gates plus a bidirectional census that fails CI on drift) would make "zero hand-written unsafe in the 10 forbid crates" a merge gate rather than a recountable assertion. Payoff: the unsafe census becomes self-maintaining across the ~30/day commit velocity, and the confinement claim upgrades from [Counted] to [CI-observed]. Complementary: franken_snowflake's "cargo-tree admissibility gate that fails the build if a forbidden crate appears in any of the ~21 feature lanes, dev included" [Verified, High] — the same fail-the-build philosophy applied to dependency hygiene for numpy's deliberately dependency-light numeric core.

**IN3 — The campaign-win result-class doctrine → from franken_whisper → why it fits → expected payoff.**
franken_whisper's "formal result-class doctrine (self-speedups are *maintenance*, not wins; a campaign win requires the actual incumbent binary running side-by-side in the same invocation with A/A nulls in [0.98, 1.02])" is the classification discipline numpy's KEEP re-recording campaign (next step 3) currently lacks. numpy's audit already estimates "~554 [of 729 non-contract claims] do compare against a live NumPy arm but predate the 2026-07-26 schema" [Maintainer claim, High] — but re-recording without a published doctrine risks re-certifying self-speedups as wins. Adopting the doctrine (self-speedup = maintenance; campaign win = same-invocation incumbent + A/A nulls in band) gives the n=300 hand-read a verdict vocabulary before it starts. Payoff: the re-recording campaign produces comparable, doctrine-graded rows instead of 729 ad-hoc judgments — and failed A/A-null campaigns get published as "no admissible verdict" (whisper's practice) rather than silently dropped.

**IN4 — Calibrated loss matrices + audit certificates for the runtime decision engine → from franken_scipy (CASP) → why it fits → expected payoff.**
numpy's `fnp-runtime` selects Allow / FullValidate / FailClosed "by a closed-form Bayesian posterior update against a fixed loss model (silent-corruption cost 100.0, false-rejection cost 125.0 — the asymmetry is the point)" [Maintainer claim, Medium]. franken_scipy's CASP does "runtime algorithm selection by Bayesian expected-loss minimization against calibrated loss matrices, with audit certificates and conformal-calibration fallback" across five numerical domains. The fit is subsystem-to-subsystem: replace numpy's fixed, asserted loss constants with calibrated loss matrices learned from the conformance corpus, and emit audit certificates per decision the way CASP does. Payoff: the dual-mode runtime's decisions become evidence-backed (calibrated) rather than asserted (fixed constants) — moving the runtime from Genesis-stage (§4.10) toward the machine-checked standard the rest of the repo already holds.

**IN5 — Truth pack + CV-gated performance ledger → from franken_tts → why it fits → expected payoff.**
franken_tts's "seam-by-seam parity apparatus — a pinned truth pack (HF weights @ `5d83992`, upstream source @ `022e286`), a discrepancies ledger, a negative-evidence ledger, and a CV≤5%-gated performance ledger" [per its packet]. numpy's contract-grade row schema (same-invocation incumbent, named host, ELF hash, dual A/A null) pins the *binary* but not the *oracle*: the 499/499 lock is host-numpy-relative (501 names on numpy 2.3.x) and the KEEP re-recordings will run against whatever numpy the fleet has. Adopting the truth-pack pattern — a pinned oracle record (numpy source pin + fixture hashes, checked in like tts's PIN_RECORD.md) plus a CV admission gate on the performance ledger — would make contract-grade rows reproducible by a third party, not just maintainer-recorded. Payoff: the re-recording campaign's output becomes independently re-runnable, which is the precondition for ever flipping revisit trigger 6 ("independent benchmark or review published").

## 7. CROSS-POLLINATION OUT — concepts from franken_numpy other projects should adopt

**OUT1 — The KEEP-claim incumbent-coverage audit + debt decomposition → to franken_mermaid, franken_networkx, frankenfs, franken_torch → why it fits → expected payoff.**
Each target already runs the deficit but lacks the audit machinery or the decomposition: franken_mermaid self-reports "225 KEEP claims, 8 with a live incumbent ratio — 96.4% without" but shows no re-recordable-vs-re-measurable triage; franken_networkx's claim-coverage audit grades "2.0% incumbent-attested" with no scoped work plan; frankenfs's "166 published benchmark rows cannot prove which machine they ran on" [Verified, High] is what the audit's row contract would have prevented; franken_torch's ledger "certifies exactly one of fourteen vs-PyTorch lanes as quotable" [Maintainer claim, High] and needs the audit to scope the other thirteen. Export numpy's full pattern: the incumbent-coverage audit doc *plus* the seeded hand-read that splits schema debt from measurement debt, so each project gets a sized work plan instead of a shame number. Payoff: four projects convert "our benchmarks are unattested" from a FATAL into a scheduled campaign with a defined done-condition.

**OUT2 — The ledger-hygiene gate (machine-checkable evidence-row contract as CI) → to frankenfs, franken_redis, franken_pandas → why it fits → expected payoff.**
frankenfs is the acute case: its worker-scope audit "admitted 166 published benchmark rows cannot prove which machine they ran on" [Verified, High] — G2's `host=`/`worker=`/`harness=`/ELF-hash contract rules that failure out at write time. franken_redis's reality-check ledger documents a project "red across the board" just before its pin; machine-checkable rows would make its recovery auditable instead of asserted. franken_pandas already pins "ELF SHA" on certified lanes [Code-verified, High] — the hygiene gate generalizes that discipline from lanes to *every* ledger row. Payoff: benchmark rows become self-describing artifacts (which machine, which binary, which harness) across the three projects most exposed to provenance challenges.

**OUT3 — Differential-against-live-oracle conformance on every CI run → to franken_networkx, franken_sympy, franken_pandas → why it fits → expected payoff.**
franken_networkx's surface is 181-functions delegation-backed with a README-admitted FFI tax ("`G[u][v]` 0.36× ... `remove_node` 0.0037× at 25.6k nodes"); a live-oracle differential harness (numpy-style: oracle captured per CI run, drift matrices, divergence ledger) would quantify that tax continuously instead of in prose. franken_sympy's admission story is fixture-based ("230/230" with the Sep-8 diagnostic that "229/230 fixtures differ on the exact-surface comparator") — a live-SymPy-oracle differential would generalize beyond fixtures. franken_pandas's packet already names "differential conformance" as exemplar methodology; numpy's contribution is the *operational* pattern: oracle-on-every-CI-run plus a 0-active-row divergence ledger as the standing quality bar. Payoff: three delegation-heavy projects get a machine-checked parity signal that updates with the incumbent instead of rotting between audits.

**OUT4 — The tier-attributed surface model + severance experiment → to franken_networkx, franken_sympy, franken_torch → why it fits → expected payoff.**
franken_networkx already maintains a delegation ledger (and its two ledgers *disagree*: "the export-route appendix classifies 763 `__all__` rows with **0** NX_DELEGATED, while the divergence ledger counts **181** intentionally-delegated functions" — "different denominators and detection rules"); a numpy-style tier model (native / binding / identity-delegated) plus a severance build would resolve the disagreement into one number. franken_sympy's "no upstream fallback" posture is undercut by its 4 `import sympy` delegation sites in the dev shell [Code-verified, High] — severance turns the posture into a measured fraction. franken_torch's "oracle mirror is absent" and its 1-of-14 quotable lanes need exactly this accounting structure. Payoff: the program's three most delegation-exposed projects get an honest, numeric native-vs-delegation split — the same number numpy's own kill test is designed to produce.

**OUT5 — The counted unsafe-site census method → to asupersync → why it fits → expected payoff.**
asupersync's packet: "`deny(unsafe_code)` at the root is real, but 47 files carry `#[allow(unsafe_code)]` — platform probes via `libc::fallocate`, x86..." — the islands are *named* but not *bounded by count*. numpy's method (strict regex census over all `.rs` files excluding comments, per-crate attribution, confinement verification — "0 unsafe sites in all crates except `fnp-python`; the 642 strict-counted sites, all in `fnp-python`" [Counted, High]) would give asupersync a machine-repeatable island census: exact site count, per-file attribution, and a number that can be ratcheted down. Payoff: the suite's most security-sensitive runtime (epoll/io_uring, TLS, QUIC) gets the same auditable unsafe accounting numpy already publishes — and a ratchet metric for future work.
