## 1. OWN-PACKET DISTILLATION

- **Verdict:** "a **genuinely substantive, unusually self-auditing clean-room Rust reimplementation of the Mermaid diagram pipeline**" [Inference, High] — 9 crates, 258,093 first-party Rust lines / 338 files, 27 diagram types (28-variant enum), 18+Auto layout algorithms (19-variant enum), four render backends (SVG, terminal, Canvas2D, WASM) fed by one shared IR [all Verified, High].
- **TRL:** 6. "Prototype demonstrated in a relevant environment: tagged release, live public WASM demo, 2,520 tests + 53 golden pairs + fuzz targets — but no production deployment, no published package, no independent validation. (Why 6 and not 5: the prototype is public and live, not lab-only. Why not 7: no operational-environment demonstration by any user.)"
- **NODUS ring:** Explore — "Under the ring-down rule ('when in doubt, ring down, not up'), Explore is the honest ring for a substantive-but-unproven project: the release artifact is a source-only tag with no binaries and no registry publication, the CI-snapshot workload fit is argued rather than evidenced by any user, and independent validation is zero."
- **Strongest strength:** the adversarial evidence discipline — "the repo publishes its losses, its rejected experiments, and an audit of its own unmeasured claims." Concretely: a 726-row negative-evidence perf ledger with mandatory A/A nulls and ELF-SHA-256 provenance, a machine-readable `**Legacy incumbent arm (same invocation):**` marker contract, and a CLAIM_COVERAGE_AUDIT.md self-reporting "225 KEEP claims, 8 with a live incumbent ratio — 96.4% without" [all Verified, High].
- **Strongest ceiling:** "**bus factor 1 at extreme velocity**" — one human maintainer, ~3,282 commits in ~7 months (~15/day), on a pinned `nightly-2026-08-31`, with unobservable quality gates: "if the maintainer stops, the 258,093-line tree rots within quarters regardless of how good the benchmarks are" [Verified, High on counts/velocity; Inference, High on the rot conclusion].
- **The 3 most important pieces of evidence:**
  1. **Zero-`unsafe` census** — "all 9 crate roots carry `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` and a code-level census finds zero actual `unsafe` tokens (the 29 non-comment hits are XSS-filter test names about 'unsafe URLs')" [Verified, High]; "the strongest unsafe posture in this program" [analyst comparative claim, Verified, High]. Even `mimalloc`'s `#[global_allocator]` is safe-Rust by language rule, "as the code comment itself explains."
  2. **The crash table (honest non-claims)** — "mermaid-js 11.15.0 throws `RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded` on all seven pinned 2,000–10,000-node workloads; frankenmermaid renders all seven," recorded as `CANNOT` with "no speedup ratio derived — 'a figure derived from a wall-clock budget would be an invented number'" [Maintainer claim with documented method, Medium].
  3. **The claim-coverage audit** — self-reports "225 KEEP claims, 8 with a live incumbent ratio — 96.4% without" (2026-07-30 snapshot), including the bd-4isi episode "where the project proved *itself* was timing a partial render against mermaid's full one" [Verified, High].
- **Caveat that must travel with the packet:** the headline ≈871× median vs mermaid-js is "the best-documented number in the repo, but the corpus definition, harness scripts, and host (maintainer-side multi-worker hosts) are maintainer-side; an independent rerun needs the harness + pinned bundle + equivalent hardware — plausible but unattempted" [Maintainer claim with strong documented method, Medium], and it compares "native batch Rust on server-class hardware vs a browser DOM renderer in Chromium."

## 3. CI/EVIDENCE BAR: WHAT FRANKENMERMAID DEMONSTRATES THAT OTHERS LACK

Be precise: the claim-governance *vocabulary* (KEEP/REJECT ledgers, incumbent-arm markers, claim-coverage audits) is **fleet-wide** — franken_networkx runs a 37,116-line negative-evidence ledger with a pre-commit-enforced INCUMBENT-vs-SELF-SPEEDUP verdict contract and its own claim-coverage audit (2.0% incumbent-attested); franken_numpy's fleet audit (`docs/KEEP_CLAIM_INCUMBENT_COVERAGE.md`, 2026-07-31) found 729 of 751 KEEP claims lacking same-invocation incumbent ratios; franken_tts uses do-not-retry predicates (NE-002, NE-003). Against that background, what mermaid has that named others demonstrably lack:

1. **A code-level zero-`unsafe` census with comment-vs-code-vs-string classification, across all 9 crate roots, with the compiler closing the macro-expansion hole.** Mechanism: `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` at every crate root plus an exhaustive census finding "zero `unsafe` tokens (the 29 non-comment hits are XSS-filter test names about 'unsafe URLs')" [Verified, High], with the hook-audit defense that the forbid gate "closes the hole a token census alone would leave" because "the compiler rejects `unsafe` introduced by macro expansion too." The packet claims "the strongest unsafe posture in this program" and "no other packet in this program verified a cleaner unsafe census" [Verified, High]. Honest caveat: that comparative claim was written without seeing the other packets, and **franken_lean has a 697-row `ci/UNSAFE_LEDGER.txt` with "a bidirectional CI census that passed at the pin" [CI-observed, High]** — i.e., lean's unsafe discipline is CI-enforced per commit while mermaid's is census-verified once. Mermaid's census is the deeper single-shot proof; lean's is the more mechanized recurring one. (Others with forbid gates — franken_markdown, franken_agent_detection, franken_alignment, franken_drone — did not execute a hit-classified census.)
2. **A structural-equivalence oracle for head-to-head claims — geometry-typed, not pixel diffs.** Mechanism: the head-to-head harness checks "4 gating invariants (rendered-text token containment one-directional, node-ID set equality, cross-engine edge topology, per-engine topology vs input graph) with 5 mutation controls + 2 negative controls on its self-test; explicitly *not* a pixel diff ('a pixel diff would report a large distance between two correct renders')" [Maintainer claim with documented method, Medium]. Others lack it: franken_manim's fidelity story is "bit-locked self-goldens as merge blockers" plus a human-judged Look Gallery [Maintainer claim, Medium] — self-referential, not cross-engine; franken_markdown uses byte-identical self-goldens "entirely maintainer-generated" with "CI fails if it drops" wording the packet calls stale.
3. **The crash-table empirical anchor with an explicit non-claim taxonomy (`CANNOT` / `DNF-timeout`, no ratios derived).** Mechanism: maintainer reproduces mermaid-js's `RangeError` per workload (wall times to failure 6.5 s–625.4 s) and "the README refuses to derive a ratio from a time budget" [Maintainer claim with documented method, Medium]. No other packet anchors its benchmark story on incumbent *failures* rather than ratios; franken_networkx's Gauntlet and franken_numpy's ledgers are ratio stories, and mermaid's own packet notes "A crash is binary and the least gameable number in the packet."
4. **An `evidence` release-signoff binary implementing gate-aggregation with an override-authorization protocol** (2,840-line `evidence.rs`) — no other packet has a signoff protocol of this form. Honest caveat: it is *unconsumed* — "no CI workflow consumes" it and the gates file it would aggregate has no workflow [Verified, High]. It is capability, not practice.
5. **A `RolloutPhase` canary state machine** (`Disabled → Canary → Partial → Full → RolledBack` in `fm-core/src/canary.rs`) for feature rollout [Verified mechanism, High]. No other packet ships a generalized rollout state machine (frankenfs's "soak/canary" is a readiness lane, not a mechanism; franken_agent_detection's "fixture canary" is an upstream-format tripwire — a different concept sharing only the word).

## 6. CROSS-POLLINATION IN (other packets → frankenmermaid)

1. **CI-enforced unsafe-site ledger** → *origin: franken_lean* ("697-row `ci/UNSAFE_LEDGER.txt` with a bidirectional CI census that passed at the pin" [CI-observed, High]). **Why it fits:** mermaid's zero-`unsafe` is census-verified once (by the analyst), but the packet's own §4.4 flags a hole — the README claims "CI workflow per push" while *no workflow consumes the gates* [Verified, High]; one `std::collections::HashSet` already survived in the e-graph path contradicting the docs. **Expected payoff:** the headline claim becomes machine-guarded per commit — a ledger census in CI would have caught the HashSet sentence going false before the analyst did, and it closes the macro-expansion/introduced-unsafe hole on every future commit.
2. **Fail-closed verification lanes (refuse with exit 3 rather than return a false green)** → *origin: frankengit* ("dormant verification lanes that refuse with exit 3 instead of returning a false green" [Verified, High]; "a genesis-stage method no incumbent forge ships"). **Why it fits:** mermaid's `evidence` release-signoff binary and head-to-head harness currently emit judgments in a maintainer-run, CI-less environment where provenance gaps are invisible; the packet notes the 11 gates "have no visible enforcement point." **Expected payoff:** wire exit-3 refusal into the signoff protocol so a release-signoff *refuses* when ELF provenance or the A/A null is missing — converting an unconsumed binary into a trustable gate.
3. **Pre-commit ledger verdict gate + periodic ledger resurrection/re-adjudication** → *origin: franken_networkx* (the ledger verdict contract "enforced by the pre-commit gate `scripts/perf_ledger_preflight.py`" [Maintainer claim, High]; "the six-class LEDGER_RESURRECTION taxonomy (1,044 entries re-adjudicated)" [Counted, High], "adopted fleet-wide from frankenfs"). **Why it fits:** mermaid's 96.4% audit is a single 2026-07-30 snapshot and its §4.10 already warns the audit is a "one-time performance, not a discipline" if the cadence lapses; as its 225 KEEP rows gain incumbent arms, rows need re-adjudication. **Expected payoff:** the audit becomes a living gate — pre-commit enforcement blocks new non-contract claims, and resurrection re-adjudicates rows as the harness improves.
4. **Anchor-bound upstream contracts with drift-failing CI** → *origin: franken_lean* ("kernel rules anchored to upstream source lines with `expect=` tokens that fail CI on drift" [Verified, High]). **Why it fits:** mermaid's Chromium-measured fidelity anchors (node shapes, ARIA roledescription) are pinned to mermaid-js 11.15.0; when the pin moves, the hard-coded measurements silently go stale — exactly the README-drift pattern in reverse. **Expected payoff:** re-measurement becomes mandatory at pin bumps; the fidelity anchoring stays a renewable claim instead of a one-time snapshot.
5. **Registry-invariant build-failing tests** → *origin: franken_agent_detection* ("registry-invariant tests that fail the build on slug drift" [Code-verified, High]; "the evidence discipline … is the best in the program" for a distributed crate). **Why it fits:** mermaid already compiler-pins one capability row per layout variant (`layout_algorithm_rows()`), but the packet's own drift catalogue (shapes 23→52, arrows 30→47, themes 10→11, lines 114k→258k [Verified, High]) is entirely in the counts the registry-invariant pattern covers. **Expected payoff:** README-vs-code drift becomes a build failure at commit time instead of an analyst's §4.4 finding.
