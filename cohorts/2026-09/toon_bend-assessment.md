## Hook

The claim is in the repository's name line: a byte-for-byte port of the `toon` JSON/TOON converter from Rust to Bend 2, a language with no `f64`, no mutual recursion and a built-in proof checker. The claim can be checked from a clone, and it held on a platform the README calls untested: the analyst rebuilt the Rust original from its public commit, confirmed that it reproduces the committed goldens byte for byte, then ran the Bend port against the same goldens and against the rebuilt original on fresh fuzzed inputs. Both agreed everywhere except ten cases that write to Linux's `/dev/full`, which macOS does not have and where the original and the port fail identically [Analyst-run, High]. The costs are specific: no package, a compiler checkout pinned eleven releases behind upstream, and about 4× and 25× the original's user CPU time on two inputs on this analyst's noisy host (the maintainer's gated reference run puts the port at about 6× the pinned original's wall time and about 10× an `opt-level=3` build, geometric mean of 66 cells).

## TL;DR

**Verdict.** toon_bend is a feature-complete port of one small CLI (`toon_rust` 0.2.4's command-line surface) whose parity evidence the analyst could rerun end to end: a 1078-case golden corpus captured from the pinned original, a sha256 manifest, a differential fuzzer that runs against the original, and 616 laws that Bend's checker accepts, 295 of which restate captured goldens through the pure core. The analyst reproduced the golden verdict on three executor lanes and a sample of the fourth, reproduced the goldens themselves from an independently rebuilt original, and ran the differential runner with zero differences [Analyst-run, High]. It is not a product anyone would install today: no release, no CI, Linux-only by its own statement, a hand-cloned compiler at a pinned commit, and throughput far below the Rust original. Its value is as a worked example of how to port a program to a proof-checked language with evidence that survives an outside rerun. **TRL 5. NODUS: Explore-with-a-ceiling. CI C4. Release R1. License: Rider. Bus factor 1.**

## Did you know?

The goldens are not only compared by the harness; 295 of them are also restated as laws of the form `C.run_pure(argv, bytes) == (exit code, stdout, stderr)` in `port/LAWS.bend` and discharged by Bend's own normalizer when `PROOF.bend` is checked [Counted, High for the count; Code-verified, High for the form]. That gives those cases a second execution path, the checker's evaluator, independent of the C and JavaScript back ends. The price is that the proof gate took 818 s and 5.17 GB peak RSS on the analyst's host [Analyst-run, High].

toon_bend is a Bend 2 reimplementation of the `toon` CLI (JSON to TOON and back, with token statistics), written from a 716-clause behavioral specification of the original whose clauses cite the original's source lines and were checked by running it, and judged byte for byte against 1078 captured cases [Maintainer claim for the method; Counted, High for the case count]. ## 4.4 Architecture (reconstructed)

**Program.** Ten Bend files, 13,932 lines in `port/` [Counted, High]: `LAWS.bend` 2,747 and `PROOF.bend` 2,557 (laws and proofs, not imported by `main.bend`), and 8,612 lines of program in eight files: `decode.bend` 2,561, `cli.bend` 1,407, `f64.bend` 1,347, `json.bend` 1,208, `encode.bend` 897, `text.bend` 540, `bignat.bend` 412, `main.bend` 240; plus `stdin_open.c` (11) and `stdin_open.js` (5), the two twins of the one custom effect [Counted, High]. For scale, the oracle's `src/` is 7,742 lines of Rust at `694d73b` [Counted, High], and that Rust sits on serde_json and clap, whose messages and number algorithms the port re-implements. Data flow per README: strict UTF-8, a byte-stepping JSON pushdown reader into one self-recursive `Json` type, annotate, emit TOON; the reverse path scans all lines first, then a line-stepping decoder, then one JSON writer [Maintainer claim, Medium; module names match the files, Code-verified, High]. Bend's restrictions (no mutual recursion, `match` only on parameters) shape the code: one recursive def per traversal, verdicts computed by callers [Maintainer claim, Medium].

**Harness.** 46 files in `scripts/`; with `cases/*.py`, 10,455 lines, nearly all Python, shell and TypeScript [Counted, High]. `scripts/ORIGIN.md` says the harness was copied from an external "porting-to-bend2" skill on 2026-09-20 and that ten scripts have since diverged from the skill's copies [Maintainer claim, High]. The load-bearing pieces: `case_manifest.py` (capture, floor, conform; checks the oracle's identity by sha256), `lanes.sh` (four lanes, MISSING is not PASS), `diff-fuzz.py` (seven lenses against the original), `hand-mutants.py` (do the laws bite), `converge.sh` and `claims-audit.py` (the governance gates). The CLI reading of an `oracle/` binary that is gitignored means every oracle-side script needs the user to build the original first [Code-verified, High].

**Corpus.** `goldens/cases.tsv`: 1078 unique case names, generated by `cases/build-cases.py` from hand cases and the TOON spec's fixtures; `goldens/` holds 3234 files (`.out`, `.err`, `.exit` per case) plus `MANIFEST.txt` and `MANIFEST.prev.txt` [Counted, High]. The MANIFEST's provenance line records the capture host's paths (`/data/projects/toon_bend/oracle/toon`) and the executable's sha256 `821287ea…` [Verified, High]. Every one of the 12 commits that touched golden files also touched the MANIFEST [Git-observed, High]; that is consistency of the capture tool, not independent proof, which is what the oracle rebuild in claim 2 supplies.

**Unsafe and dependencies.** `import Base` only; no third-party Bend library [Maintainer claim, Medium; no other import was seen in the files read, Code-verified, Medium]. No `@unsafe` in program files [Counted, High]. The native binary depends on the Bend runtime emitted into C; the JS build runs under bun [Code-verified, Medium].

**asupersync.** Not a dependency of toon_bend, which has no Rust code [Verified absence, High]. The oracle has an optional `asupersync = "=0.5.0"` behind its `async-stream` feature, off in the pinned build; the port excludes that feature as out of scope (`PLAN` §3) [Code-verified, High].

**Agent authorship.** 234 commits over five days (48, 15, 55, 55, 61 per day); 233 authored under the handle Dicklesworthstone and one under "Claude (wip)"; 219 carry a Claude Opus co-author trailer (147 "Claude Opus 5 (1M context)", 72 "Claude Opus 5.5 (1M context)") [Git-observed, High]. `PORT_STATE.md` names the author of phases −1 to 5 as a Claude Code session [Maintainer claim, High].

Outputs were byte-identical (sha256 prefixes `7f28c487…` and `f44011cd…` for both programs) [Analyst-run, Medium for the ratios, High for identity]. Direction agrees with the maintainer; the tabular ratio (about 3.7× CPU here, 8.7× in the maintainer's older capture) does not, and this rerun is not controlled enough to settle it.

**Reproduction cost.** Repository clone 6.5 MB (API `size` 6452 KB); Bend checkout 206 MB; bun and clang; about 1 minute to build the native binary; 14 minutes and 5 to 6 GB of RAM for the proof; a Rust nightly (`nightly-2026-08-31`) or RCH to rebuild the oracle [Analyst-run, High].

## 4.6 Comparison: who owns the lane

The lane is "convert JSON to TOON and back." It is owned by the format's reference implementation, `toon-format/toon` (TypeScript, 25,419 stars), with official ports in Python (769), Java (155), Go (148), Rust (`toon-format/toon-rust`, 179) and others [External, High]. The maintainer's own `toon_rust` (28 stars, released through v0.2.4 with macOS, Linux and Windows binaries) is this port's oracle and beats it on every axis a user would weigh: speed, packaging, platforms, maturity [External, High for releases; Inference, High for the comparison]. toon_bend does not compete in that lane and does not claim to; its README's comparison table lists the Rust tool as "the oracle of this port." The lane it occupies is different: a published, fully evidenced port of a real CLI into Bend 2, a language whose proposition is that laws and proofs constrain what AI agents write. No other TOON implementation carries checker-accepted laws, and no other public Bend 2 program of this size with an oracle-backed golden corpus was found in the analyst's search [Inference, Low: search recall for a four-day-old ecosystem is thin; a sibling `beads_bend` exists in the same account].

## 4.7 Technical merit and adversarial review

**Strengths.** (1) The parity claim is reproducible by an outsider, including the oracle: a rebuilt original matched 1068 of 1078 goldens and the port matched the same set, on an OS the README calls untested [Analyst-run, High]. `synthesis/00-overview.md` counts analyst behavioral reproduction in 1 of the pinned 44 (`franken_threed`); here the analyst also rebuilt the oracle and checked the goldens against it. (2) The repository separates what is proved, golden-tested and measured, and writes down negatives: no quantified `fast == spec` law, 18 refused performance captures, HOLD rather than SHIP, 17 unclean review rounds [Maintainer claim, High; the file counts are Counted, High]. (3) The engineering inside Bend's limits is real: a software binary64 over big naturals that matched the Rust original on every number the analyst threw at it (2400 fuzzed literals, 20,000 doubles, the corpus), pushdown parsers shaped to pass a termination checker, and a C/JS effect twin to read descriptor 0 because Base cannot [Analyst-run, High for the matches; Code-verified, High for the effect]. (4) Divergences are registered rather than hidden: 15 DISC entries with class and scope, each a property of the Bend runtime or of speed [Maintainer claim, High].

**Weaknesses.** (1) No CI and no release: every green line is pasted by hand from maintainer sessions, and `CONTRIBUTING.md` says so ("There is NO continuous integration here") [Verified, High]. (2) The oracle is not independent of the porter. The seven `toon_rust` bug-fix commits that became the pin were made in 40 minutes on 2026-09-22 by the same agent-assisted workflow, at the owner's order to fix rather than reproduce bugs; the port then re-captured 486 goldens from the fixed original [Git-observed, High; Maintainer claim, High]. That is the right engineering choice, but it means "byte-for-byte with the original" is parity with an original that the porting process edited, and the TOON spec fixtures in the corpus contribute inputs only, with expected outputs taken from the Rust binary, not from the spec [Maintainer claim, High]. Conformance to the TOON format therefore rests on `toon_rust`'s own correctness, which this packet did not assess. (3) The Bend dependency is heavy and moving: a compiler run from a git checkout under bun, pinned to `15ae0c8` while upstream shipped 11 releases in five days; an 8 TiB address-space reservation; `--` required before the original's command line unless the launcher `bin/toon` is used; a CLI that sends a daily GET to Bend's check endpoint unless `BEND_NO_TELEMETRY=1` is set (`bend2/main.ts` lines 130 to 137) [Code-verified, High]. (4) The "non-author" reviewers are subagents launched from the maintainer's sessions on the same host and the same oracle binary [Maintainer claim, High, from `round-22.md`'s header]; they are a cross-check, not independent validation under Rulebook §2. (5) The governance apparatus now generates much of what blocks it: of the six counted findings in rounds 20 to 22, four were `converge.sh` or `claims-audit.py` misreporting and two were mutants that survived corpus and proof; none was a difference from the original, and convergence stays blocked [Maintainer claim, High; Inference, Medium for the reading].

**Bear-case steelman.** toon_bend matters to almost nobody who needs TOON. The format has a reference implementation with 25,000 stars and official ports in every mainstream language, and the maintainer's own Rust port is released, cross-platform, about 6× faster on the maintainer's gated corpus and up to 151× faster on number-heavy input. The Bend port cannot be installed without cloning a compiler at a pinned commit, runs only on Linux by its own statement, reserves 8 TiB of address space, is barred to two of the largest AI labs by license, and has three stars and no filed issues. Its proofs cover gates, first-failure rules and 295 restated goldens, not the numeric core that makes it slow. As a product it is a slower copy of a tool its author already ships. As a demonstration, its audience is one young language's early adopters, and that language shipped eleven releases in the five days after the port pinned it.

## 4.11 Trajectory (12 / 24 / 60 months) **[Inference]**

**Base case.** The repository stays a finished demonstration. The convergence count keeps HOLD until the maintainer either declares it or moves on; the Bend pin ages as upstream releases continue; nothing is packaged. Its later value is as a reference for `porting-to-bend2` and for Bend's own documentation of what the language can host.

**Upside.** Bend 2 gains users and wants flagship programs; a released toon_bend with CI becomes one of them, and its oracle-backed corpus becomes the template other Bend ports copy. That needs a release, CI, a Bend upgrade through `version-drift.sh`, and one outside party building it.

**Decay.** The pinned Bend checkout stops building on current bun or clang; `/tmp/bend` instructions break; the goldens remain valid (they describe `toon_rust`), but the port can no longer be built from the documented recipe. At 60 months the repository is readable evidence and not runnable software.

**Revisit triggers.** (1) A tagged release with binaries. (2) A CI workflow with a public verdict at a pin. (3) Two consecutive clean rounds, or a stated end to the rounds. (4) A Bend upgrade from 2.0.16 with `version-drift.sh` output published. (5) Any outside party building and running the conformance lanes, or filing an issue. (6) A change to the rider or the contribution policy.

## 4.12 Limitations and open questions

**Not done.** The Linux x86_64 lanes, which the maintainer gates on, were not run; the macOS result stands beside theirs, it does not replace it. The interpreter lane ran on 24 of 1078 cases. `hand-mutants.py` (whether the laws kill planted bugs; the maintainer's runs take 1 to 3.5 hours), `stdio-probe.py` (descriptor states), `floor.sh` (oracle determinism), `claims-audit.py`, `converge.sh`, `clean-build-check.sh` and `incumbent-bench.sh` were not run. Build reproducibility was not checked. No controlled benchmark was captured; the analyst's timings are orientation under host load 37 to 46. The Bend compiler, its C runtime and its checker were not audited, so "All terms check." is trusted as the checker's verdict and not as a proof of the checker. The DISC register was read at the heading level; the 17 review reports were not read beyond round 22's header and findings table. `toon_rust`'s conformance to the TOON spec was not assessed. The one Python eval kernel crash during the first conformance attempt was environmental (the same run then passed under bash) and produced no result used here.

**Open questions that would most change the verdict.** (1) Does anyone other than the maintainer's sessions build and use it? (2) Will the maintainer publish a release and CI, or leave the repository as a finished demonstration? (3) How much of the port's cost (about 6× the original on the maintainer's gated corpus, up to 151× on doubles) is the Bend runtime's allocation model versus the absence of `f64`, and would a future Bend with native floats close it? (4) Do the laws bite on this pin: would a fresh hand mutant outside `hand-mutants.py`'s inventory survive both the corpus and the proof? Rounds 17 to 21 each found such survivors [Maintainer claim, High].

## The eight deepening questions

1. **Provenance.** Each golden records who produced it through `goldens/MANIFEST.txt`: a capture timestamp, the original's argv, the executable's absolute path and sha256, the case file's sha256, and a sha256 per output file, plus a `recapture:` line naming the reason [Verified, High]. The attestation is not portable as it stands, because the executable it names is a private build that the plan says is not byte-reproducible across workers. What made it portable in practice was the commit pin: rebuilding `694d73b` gave a different sha256 but the same bytes on 1068 of 1078 cases. Recording the rebuild recipe and the expected per-case hashes, rather than one executable hash, would make the attestation checkable by anyone without trusting a binary.

2. **The embeddable unit.** Two units are adoptable without the port. The golden corpus (`goldens/` plus `cases/`) is a 1078-case behavioral specification of `toon` 0.2.4 that any TOON implementation could run against; cost: reading `cases.tsv`'s format and accepting that the expected outputs are `toon_rust`'s, not the spec's. The harness (`case_manifest.py`, `conform.sh`, `lanes.sh`, `diff-fuzz.py`) is language-neutral and worked on a platform the README calls untested; cost: Python 3.11 or later, and the rider. The Bend code itself is embeddable only in another Bend program.

3. **Unexercised option value.** Bend's GPU back end is unused: no bang is placed, so there is no GPU lane, and the README argues text with data-dependent structure is a poor fit [Maintainer claim, High]. One parallel `let` exists (the encoder's number pre-pass above one thread) [Maintainer claim, Medium]. The larger unused option is the checker: universally quantified `fast == spec` laws for the fast twins do not exist because the normalizer does not finish on them. If a future Bend can check those, the port's speed levers would move from "golden-tested" to "proved," which is the plan's original promise (`PLAN` §1 amendment).

4. **Benchmark honesty.** The conformance numbers survived an independent rerun on another OS, which is the load-bearing part of the thesis. The maintainer's performance numbers are gated, labeled and mostly negative; the analyst's uncontrolled rerun agrees on direction (the port is several times to tens of times slower) and not on magnitude. None of the performance numbers is load-bearing for the port's claim, and the README does not pretend otherwise: the speed story is "several times the original's work," stated first.

5. **The governance path.** There is none in view: one maintainer, no outside contributions, a harness owned by an external skill, and a delegation that lets agents approve divergences. What breaks first if velocity decays is the build recipe, not the evidence: the port depends on a Bend checkout pinned to one commit, run through bun, and on nightly Rust for the oracle. The goldens and laws would stay true descriptions of the code; the ability to rebuild would rot. A credible path would start with CI, then a release, then a Bend upgrade, all within the maintainer's control.

6. **The license as strategy.** The rider excludes OpenAI, Anthropic, their affiliates and their agents, and any use of the software in ML training or evaluation datasets and pipelines; it does not exclude users of those labs' models. For this repository the exclusion has a specific cost: Bend's stated purpose is to let people constrain AI-written code with laws and proofs, and the most obvious consumers of a proved, golden-tested reference port are evaluation harnesses for coding models, which the rider forbids for everyone. The commit history shows the port was written with Claude models; the rider does not bar that, since it restricts the named companies and ML-pipeline use, not tool users. Whether the exclusion serves the mission depends on whether the mission is the port or the method; for the method, it narrows the audience that could test it.

7. **Agent-era fit.** The concrete agent workload is porting: an agent asked to move a small CLI into a proof-checked language, with an oracle to run. For that workload this repository is a better reference than any incumbent TOON implementation, because it shows the full loop from spec extraction to golden capture to laws to registered divergences, with evidence an outsider can rerun. What would have to become true first: the method must be public and usable without the maintainer's external skills (the harness says it was copied from one), and Bend must be stable enough that a pin does not fall eleven releases behind in five days.

8. **The kill test.** Run the committed corpus against a fresh rebuild of the original and against the port on the maintainer's own platform, Linux x86_64, from a clean clone following only the README. If the rebuilt original does not reproduce the goldens, the byte-for-byte claim collapses at its foundation; if the port fails cases the original passes, parity is disproven. This packet ran that test on macOS arm64 and the claim survived, with ten platform-device cases failing identically on both programs. The Linux half remains unrun by anyone outside the maintainer's sessions.

## Cross-cutting lenses

- **Decoupling.** The port decouples a program's behavior from its implementation language: behavior is fixed by the oracle's captured outputs, and the implementation is free to be in a language with no `f64`. It also decouples "is it correct" (goldens, laws) from "is it fast" (a separate, gated ledger) [Inference, Medium].
- **Methodology export.** If the port fails as software, what survives is the oracle-golden method with its two named equivalences (original == spec, spec == fast), the discrepancy register, and the convergence rule. The analyst's rerun is evidence that the method produces claims an outsider can check; the rounds table is evidence that its gates can consume the effort meant for the product [Inference, Medium].
- **asupersync.** Verified absent from toon_bend; present only as an optional, disabled feature of the oracle, and excluded from the port's scope (§4.4) [Code-verified, High].
- **Rider.** Quoted in §4.8; strategic cost assessed in deepening question 6.
